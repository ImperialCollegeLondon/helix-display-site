# Helix Publication Summaries — user guidance

## What it is for

- Creating accessible summaries of Helix work and publications, to share back with participants, collaborators and the public (for example on the website, in newsletters, or at involvement and engagement events).
- Keeping a central, accessible record of the Centre's outputs, so the team, our partners and our funders can see and understand each other's work.

## What it is not for

- This tool does not replace the role of a lay advisor or public contributor in planning studies, putting together funding applications, or giving strategic input through a project. It is for sharing work that is already done, and for making that work easier to read.
- It is not a substitute for reading your own paper. The summary is published under Helix's name, so the accuracy of it rests with you.

## Quality control

- The AI assistant is given background on the Helix Centre, our themes and projects, and the house style for these summaries. It is not a subject expert in your field.
- Always read the output yourself before submitting it. Check three things in particular: that the findings are stated accurately, that the caveats the paper makes (small sample, single site, early stage) have not been dropped, and that anything a member of the public would not understand has been explained.
- If the work was a collaboration, check that the description of who did what is right. The assistant is told to ask, but it can only work with what it is told.

## The three steps

You do not need to use all three. If you have already written a summary, skip to step 2.

### 1. Create a summary (using DAIsy)

**What this is for:** to help you quickly draft a summary from a PDF of your paper, a report, or a file describing a project.

**Link:** [ADD DAISY AGENT LINK]

The assistant will ask you four questions: what you are summarising, which theme it belongs to, whether the work was led by Helix or Helix contributed, and then for the paper itself. It will then give you a one or two sentence summary and a longer one of 300 to 500 words. Ask it to adjust anything you are not happy with.

Have the **full paper as a PDF** ready to upload. The assistant can work from an abstract, but the summary will be noticeably thinner, because abstracts leave out the detail that makes a summary readable.

Because the tool sits inside the Imperial system, work in progress and unpublished work can be uploaded safely.

*Note: DAIsy is currently only available to people with an Imperial email address. If you do not have access, contact [ADD ACCESS CONTACT].*

*If you would rather use a different AI tool, [ADD LINK TO BRIEF PDF] describes how the assistant is set up and can be given to another model as background.*

### 2. Submit the summary

**What this is for:** submitting your summary, whether you wrote it yourself or drafted it with the assistant and edited it, so it appears on the publications page.

**Link:** https://imperial.eu.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_9LdgMtnBVbjZ1gq
[ADD PASSWORD IF THE FORM IS PASSWORD PROTECTED]

As well as the two summaries, the form asks for:

- the title, and the date of publication
- the theme and the project it relates to
- whether the work was led by Helix or Helix contributed
- the full author list, and any acknowledgements (optional)
- a link to the full paper or work
- up to five keywords
- an image to display alongside it, such as a chart, illustration or photograph (optional)

An export of all responses can be produced from Qualtrics if you need one; contact Tori.

### 3. View the summary

**Link:** https://helixcentre.com/publications

A central, public record of the Centre's outputs. Useful for reporting to participants and funders, and for finding out what colleagues are working on.

Summaries appear on the page within about fifteen minutes of submission. Each one gets a permanent short reference, so you can link straight to a particular summary: for example `helixcentre.com/publications#001`. That reference never changes, so it is safe to use in emails, slides and reports.

## Notes on process

- **Who submits:** whoever knows the work best, usually the corresponding author or the Helix lead on the project.
- **When:** once something is published or otherwise finished and shareable. Preprints are fine.
- **Editing after submission:** contact Tori rather than resubmitting, so the page does not end up with duplicates.
- **Removing something:** also contact Tori. Deleting the response removes it from the page at the next update.
