Patients recovering from breast cancer surgery regained arm movement faster when they could see feedback from a wearable device.

Summary

After breast cancer surgery, many people are left unsure how much to move their arm, and recovery can stall. This study tested whether giving patients feedback from a wrist-worn device would help them regain movement sooner…

Notes and open questions

Type of publication. It's on the line under the authors, with the publisher: "Academic paper · European Journal of Surgical Oncology". That line already exists in the code, so if it isn't showing for you, the likely cause is that the detail-page changes haven't been pushed yet rather than anything missing — worth checking before I change anything.

"View on publisher site". Sitting above the keywords, so it's visible without scrolling. Worth deciding what it should say: the button goes to whatever was submitted as the link to the work, which for about a quarter of entries is a blog, a report on helixcentre.com, or a preprint host rather than a publisher. "View the full paper" or "Read the original" would be true of all of them; "View on publisher site" would not.

About a quarter of entries have no link at all. The button is hidden entirely in that case, which leaves a gap where other pages have a clear call to action. That's unavoidable, but worth seeing on one of those entries before deciding.

Footnote headings. The page above uses navy at 0.95rem. The jump from grey to navy does most of the work — at 1.05rem the headings start to compete with "Summary" above them, which is a real heading for a section people actually read.

One thing I'd raise: the link to the paper would now appear twice, as the button at the top and as "Full paper / work" at the bottom. That's deliberate repetition and common enough, but if you'd rather not, the bottom row can go, since the button is the more findable of the two.
