Things to weigh up

With the title untouchable, the labels are the biggest saving available. "Published" and "Theme & project" are repeated on every row and take a 104px column each time, pushing the values into a narrow strip that then wraps. On a phone the values largely explain themselves — a date looks like a date, a journal looks like a journal — which is why four of these drop the labels. Option 3 keeps them and stays the tallest.

Authors are the next biggest. Two lines of names on every row, on a screen where almost nobody reads them. Cutting to one line, or dropping them, saves about as much as removing the photo.

The 64px photo doesn't earn its space. A doorway or a bedside sensor at that size is a grey smudge. Either make it big enough to read (Option 4) or small and out of the way beside the text (Option 2).

Touch targets. "Dementia › InSleep46" as inline links sits two small targets right next to each other, easy to mis-tap. Option 5's chips are the only comfortably tappable version, which matters if you want filtering used on a phone.

If you want a recommendation: Option 2. It's near the shortest, keeps the imagery that makes the page feel like Helix rather than a reference list, and keeps publisher and date visible. Option 1 is the pick if brevity beats everything; Option 3 if you'd rather change as little as possible and just fix the alignment.
