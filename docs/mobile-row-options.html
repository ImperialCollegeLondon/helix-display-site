Things to weigh up

Labels are what make the current version long. "Publisher", "Theme & project", "Published" are repeated on every row and take a column of width each time. On a phone the values mostly explain themselves — a date looks like a date — so Options 1, 2, 4 and 5 drop the labels entirely. Option 3 keeps them and is the tallest as a result.

Clamping the title to two lines saves less than it looks. Only a few of your titles run past two lines, but those are the ones that run to four or five. It also means the full title is only visible on the detail page, which is a real cost for scanning.

The 64px thumbnail isn't doing much work on a phone. At that size a photo of a doorway or a bedside sensor reads as a grey smudge. Either make it bigger so it earns its space (Option 4), shrink it and put it beside the text (Option 2), or drop it (1 and 5).

Authors are the other big consumer. Two lines of names on every row, which few readers scan on a phone. Options 2, 4 and 5 drop or demote them.

Touch targets: inline text links like "Dementia › InSleep46" are small and sit close together. Option 5's chips are the only version comfortably tappable, which matters if filtering is something you want people doing on a phone.

If you want a recommendation: Option 2. It's close to the shortest, keeps the imagery that makes the page feel like Helix rather than a bibliography, and keeps the publisher visible. Option 1 is the one to pick if brevity beats everything.
